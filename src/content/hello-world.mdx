## The Paradox of the Portfolio

There is an old saying in the industry: *"The hardest product to ship is your own portfolio."*

Client work is easy. There are deadlines, requirements, and brand guidelines. But a personal site? It is a blank canvas with infinite possibilities. It is an identity crisis in code.

For this version of my digital garden, I decided to stop overthinking and start building. I wanted a space that felt fast, fluid, and focused on content.

## Standing on the Shoulders of Giants

I believe that good design is a conversation with those who came before us. This site heavily draws inspiration from two incredible Indonesian engineers who have set the gold standard for developer portfolios:

### 1. Theodorus Clarence
If you know [Theodorus Clarence](https://theodorusclarence.com/), you know his documentation is legendary. His approach to "documentation-first" development and his clean, accessible UI patterns were a massive influence. I tried to emulate his clarity in my blog post structure and MDX components.

### 2. Kent Andrian
[Kent Andrian's website](https://kentandrian.com/) is a masterclass in minimalism and motion. The way his site breathes—subtle micro-interactions, smooth page transitions—taught me that performance isn't just about loading speed; it's about *feeling*.

## Under the Hood: The Tech Stack

To build something worthy of these inspirations, I reached for the bleeding edge.

<Features>
  <Feature title="Next.js 15 (App Router)">
    I wanted to fully embrace the **Server Component** architecture. By moving the heavy lifting to the server, the client bundle remains incredibly small, ensuring top-tier Core Web Vitals.
  </Feature>
  <Feature title="Tailwind CSS v4">
    The new engine is lightning fast. I'm using it to build a bespoke design system without fighting against the framework. No component libraries, just pure utility-first CSS.
  </Feature>
  <Feature title="Framer Motion">
    The "Bento Grid" on the homepage isn't static. It uses layout animations to handle resizing and hover states gracefully. It turns a boring list into a tactile experience.
  </Feature>
  <Feature title="Giscus">
    For the guestbook and comments, I integrated Giscus. It uses GitHub Discussions as a database, which means no backend maintenance and a verified developer audience.
  </Feature>
</Features>

## The "Hello World" Moment

The most satisfying part of this build wasn't the complex animations or the server-side rendering. It was the moment I deployed the first version.

It wasn't perfect. The dark mode flickered. The fonts were wrong. But it was *live*.

And that is the lesson I want to embrace for 2026: **Ship.**
