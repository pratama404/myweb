## The Year of The Ship

If I had to summarize 2025 in one word, it would be **"Shipping."**

For years, I was a hoarder of side projects. I had folders full of half-baked apps, "almost done" portfolios, and ideas waiting for "the right time." In 2025, I decided that the right time is simply **now**.

I shipped **Breev**. I launched **OfficeHub**. I rewrote this portfolio three times (classic developer syndrome).

## What Went Well

### 1. Embracing Public Learning
I stopped being afraid of looking like a junior. I wrote articles about things I *just* learned. Surprisingly, these were my most popular posts. People don't want experts; they want explorers who report back from the frontier.

### 2. Consistency Over Intensity
I stopped doing 14-hour coding marathons. Instead, I committed to 2 hours of deep work every single morning. The compound effect is terrifyingly real. My output doubled while my stress halved.

### 3. Community
I started engaging more on Twitter/X and local meetups. Finding a tribe of builders who are also awake at 2 AM debugging a CSS grid issue makes this lonely journey feel like a multiplayer game.

## What Didn't Go Well

### 1. Health
I neglected the physical hardware (my body) to optimize the software. Back pain is not a badge of honor; it's a system warning. In 2026, the gym is a non-negotiable dependency.

### 2. Scope Creep
Project **Wikarta** failed. Why? Because I tried to build Google Maps, Uber, and Yelp in one app as a solo dev. I learned the hard lesson of MVP: **Minimum** Viable Product. If it's not embarrassing to launch, you launched too late.

## The Stack for 2026

I'm betting big on:
*   **Rust**: For tooling and low-level optimizations.
*   **Local AI**: Privacy-focused intelligence.
*   **Svelte 5**: I missed the simplicity of reactive inputs.

## Gratitude

Thank you to everyone who read my code, used my tools, or sent a kind DM. We are building the future, one commit at a time.

Here's to a bug-free 2026. (Who am I kidding, there will be bugs. But we'll fix them).
